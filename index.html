01 / A DIFFERENT PERSPECTIVE本

What if eating well
meant coming home?

Rice is more than a carbohydrate.
It's a language of care.

For generations, the Cantonese table has made room for grains, greens, warming soups, and shared dishes. But in a world of “cut this” and “avoid that,” our relationship with familiar foods can become complicated.

We're exploring a gentler question: how can modern nutrition and our food traditions sit at the same table?

Let's look at the whole picture ↗
02 / BEYOND THE LOW-CARB HEADLINE衡

Your roots matter.
So does the evidence.

“Asian” isn't one body type or one diet.
Personal health, food quality, and context matter more than a single rule.

01 — THE CONTEXT
◒

One diet doesn't fit all.

Some Asian populations develop type 2 diabetes at lower BMI levels. That calls for individual assessment—not a blanket rule to cut out rice.

02 — THE TRADE-OFF
↗

What replaces carbs matters.

Restricting grains, fruit, and legumes can reduce fibre and food variety. Replacing them with unsaturated fats and plant proteins differs from relying on processed meat.

03 — THE EVERYDAY
☷

Build a balanced plate.

Try vegetables, a protein source, and a portion of whole grains or other starches. Portions can be adapted to your needs, preferences, and clinical advice.

A note on the science & sources ＋

Low-carbohydrate diets may help some people manage blood glucose in the short term; long-term benefits, adherence, and food quality vary. This site does not claim that low-carb eating is uniquely harmful to all Asian people. If you take glucose-lowering medication, discuss major dietary changes with your clinician.
